About Us

Welcome to Haomics Lab, established by Xingjie Hao at Huazhong University of Science and Technology. We are an interdisciplinary research group working at the intersection of statistical genomics, multi-omics, artificial intelligence, and population health. Our overarching goal is to develop reliable, interpretable, and scalable analytical methods that translate large-scale genomic data into a better understanding of disease mechanisms, improved risk prediction, and more effective strategies for precision prevention.

Our methodological research focuses on genetic prediction and causal inference for complex traits and diseases. We develop statistical models and computational tools for polygenic risk scoring, cross-population genetic analysis, fine-mapping, gene–environment interaction, and linear and nonlinear omics association studies. By combining statistical learning and artificial intelligence with principled statistical frameworks, we aim to address key challenges arising from high-dimensional data, correlated molecular traits, population heterogeneity, and prediction uncertainty.

Our multi-omics research integrates GWAS, TWAS, PWAS, PheWAS, Mendelian randomization, colocalization, proteomics, metabolomics, epigenomics, and single-cell data. These complementary approaches allow us to trace the biological pathway from genetic variation to tissues and cell types, molecular phenotypes, and ultimately disease development. We are particularly interested in identifying tissue- and cell-type-specific mechanisms, characterizing genetic and environmental heterogeneity, and developing genetically informed prediction models for molecular traits such as proteins and metabolites.

Our research has contributed to annotation-informed polygenic risk prediction, trans-ancestry genetic studies, nonlinear proteome-wide association analysis, and the genetic characterization of complex diseases. Recent work has improved risk prediction and fine-mapping for kidney stone disease across populations, revealed nonlinear relationships between circulating proteins and venous thromboembolism, and provided genetic and multi-omics insights into cardiovascular, metabolic, urinary, neurological, and other complex disorders. Building on these foundations, we are expanding toward cell-type-resolved gene–environment interaction, uncertainty-aware molecular prediction, and integrative analyses that connect population-scale discoveries with biological mechanisms and clinical relevance.

We warmly welcome opportunities for academic and interdisciplinary collaboration, as well as motivated students who share our scientific interests and would like to grow with the lab.
